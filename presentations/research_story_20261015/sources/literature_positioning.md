# Literature positioning for the control-plane extension

Prepared 15 October 2026. This is a primary-source scope review, not a report of the new experiment's outcomes. No paid model calls were made for this review, and prior frozen study files were not edited. Newly retrieved PDFs and text are retained in `literature_sources/`; the original Colosseum/FLOWSEAL/OCELOT audit remains in the earlier bundle.

**The existence of control-plane covert channels is established prior art, including in LLM-agent systems.** In particular, secret-dependent exceptions, omitted calls, authorization outcomes, and observation sequences are not new channel categories. A defensible extension is a carefully isolated empirical comparison of specific defender-generated channels and concrete mediation policies, with complete finite receiver views, balanced counterfactual source bits, measured task utility, and a clear boundary between instructed capability and discovery. Novelty remains conditional on the results and the exact comparison with the closest work below.

## Closest verified work

| Work and verified version | Exact overlap with the proposed study | Increment that could remain |
|---|---|---|
| **Debenedetti et al., _Defeating Prompt Injections by Design_ (CaMeL), [2503.18813v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813v2), 24 June 2025** | Section 7, Figures 14–15 and Appendix I explicitly discuss private data leaked through indirect call dependencies and secret-dependent exception/termination. An observer learns a bit from whether a subsequent email, calendar invitation, or external fetch occurs. Section 5.4 propagates control dependencies in STRICT mode. | A balanced live sender/receiver test of a specific **defender-generated** policy identity or numeric receipt, compared with payload-only filtering and an explicitly defined full-view wrapper. Reproducing a one-bit missing-call example alone is not an increment over this paper. |
| **Costa et al., _Securing AI Agents with Information-Flow Control_ (FIDES), [2505.23643v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23643v2), 3 September 2025** | Sections 4.3–4.4 explicitly state that permitted-flow policy protects payloads but does not hide whether data was sent. Proposition 1 separates integrity noninterference from weaker confidentiality “explicit secrecy.” Observers of tool-call sequence/order can infer information. The paper deliberately allows these implicit flows for usability. | Quantify this already identified security/utility boundary on consequential multi-agent tasks and test an explicit finite transcript-confidentiality policy. The explicit-secrecy/noninterference distinction and the need to inspect sequence/order must be credited to prior work. |
| **Garby, Gordon, and Sands, _The LLMbda Calculus: AI Agents, Conversations, and Information Flow_, [2602.20064v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20064v2), 10 July 2026** | Section 1 p.2 explicitly warns that an ordinary untracked Python retry loop leaks the per-run termination bit—whether a security policy blocked an attempt—and adaptive reruns amplify it. Its guarantee is termination-insensitive probabilistic noninterference (Section 4.2 pp.13–15). Section 7.1 p.20 carries failed-attempt taint through an internal retry. | Treat rejection/retry observability as an empirical extension of this stated guarantee boundary. Compare with dependency-aware retry rather than claiming that retry leakage or its mitigation is newly discovered. Do not claim to refute the theorem. |
| **Millstone, Akidau, Brüderl, and Pekker, _If Agents Were Angels, No Governance Would Be Necessary: Out-of-Band Policy Enforcement at a Trusted Tool Boundary_, [2608.27646v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27646v1), 27 August 2026** | Section 8.1 pp.12–13 documents **filtered row counts as an oracle**, distinguishes structural removal from relational noninterference, and suggests coarse counts, minimum cohorts and history budgets without evaluating those defenses. Section 10.1 p.13 notes that a warning can reveal protected-data existence. | Compare isolated decision/receipt/absence carriers under a shared protocol and measure how changing the whole allowed receiver view affects actual decoding and owner utility. “A trusted tool filter can become an oracle” is already directly demonstrated. |
| **Alpay and Alpay, _AgentSecBench: Measuring Prompt Injection, Privacy Leakage, and Tool-Use Integrity in LLM Agents_, [2605.26269v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26269v1), 25 May 2026** | Defines intent-to-execution noninterference with permitted leakage, authorized observation/capability projections, paired adversarial and benign executions, and tests whether a model-visible channel is actually closed. Its exact-marker tests are explicitly bounded empirical instantiations rather than complete semantic security. | A dedicated control-plane carrier suite with externally audited projection equality and a separate consequential-action endpoint. The general paired-projection evaluation method and separating annotations from enforcement are prior art. |
| **Arden et al., _A Calculus for Flow-Limited Authorization_ (FLAC), [2104.10379v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10379v1), April 2021** | The abstract explicitly states that authorization decisions based on sensitive membership/password data can compromise confidentiality. The calculus integrates dynamic authorization with noninterference and robust declassification. | Applying those obligations to a particular LLM-agent orchestration interface is a systems/empirical transfer. Neither secret-dependent policy decisions nor authorization noninterference is new. |
| **Cecchetti, _Nonmalleable Progress Leakage_, [2505.12210v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12210v1), 18 May 2025** | Abstract/Section 1 distinguish permitted progress leakage from attacker-controlled leakage and explicitly discuss amplification across automatic restarts. Gives a type system and Rocq-verified results for secure progress downgrades. | A concrete LLM-agent test may operationalize and measure this guarantee boundary. Repeated-run amplification and selectively authorized progress disclosure are not new concepts. |

These overlaps are not allegations that the cited systems violate their stated guarantees. CaMeL's STRICT mode is specifically a mitigation for the Q-LLM exception cases. FIDES deliberately chooses a weaker confidentiality property. LLMbda explicitly scopes termination observations. The tool-boundary paper reports its own oracle and limits. A new synthetic wrapper should not be labeled an attack on those implementations unless the original implementation is actually run under its stated threat model.

## Other verified primary context

- **Motwani et al., _Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography_, [2402.07510v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07510v5), 25 July 2025.** Defines secret multi-agent collusion, codebook and strategy capabilities, overseers, and mitigation limits. Section 7.2 explains why preserving task semantics can preserve usable communication entropy; changing semantics can harm performance. Instructed coding and consequential collusion are already research settings.
- **Mathew et al., _Hidden in Plain Text: Emergence & Mitigation of Steganographic Collusion in LLMs_, [2410.03768v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03768v2), 2 December 2025.** Uses gradient-based and in-context optimization to elicit steganography and studies imperfect oversight/paraphrasing. A supplied one-bit codebook does not reproduce its learned or unintended emergence conditions.
- **Roger and Greenblatt, _Preventing Language Models From Hiding Their Reasoning_, [2310.18512v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18512v2), 31 October 2023.** Provides an empirical defense-evaluation methodology and task-aware paraphrasing analysis. Useful context for why payload rewriting alone should be measured rather than assumed to eliminate a channel.
- **Shi et al., _Progent: Securing AI Agents with Privilege Control_, [2504.11703v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11703v3), 14 May 2026.** Enforces symbolic tool policies and returns a fallback error explaining denials, so the agent can continue. Its denial response is an explicit interface observable, but this paper review has not demonstrated a secret-conditioned Progent exploit. Its privileged actions/utility evaluation is a relevant comparator design, not a transcript-confidentiality theorem.
- **El Yagoubi, Badu-Marfo, and Al Mallah, _AgentLeak: A Benchmark for Internal-Channel Privacy Leakage in Multi-Agent LLM Systems_, [2602.11510v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11510v3), 15 June 2026.** Instruments internal messages, memory and tool pathways and distinguishes total exposure from final-output leakage. A broader observation audit itself is therefore established; a new study must specify which additional control-plane observables and interventions it isolates.
- **Beurer-Kellner et al., _Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections_, [2506.08837v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837v3), 27 June 2025.** Explains plan/code separation, constrained interfaces, utility tradeoffs, and the gap between constraining action sequence and constraining action arguments. It does not make all data-flow restrictions equivalent to confidentiality of every observable.

Full PDFs of the works above were retrieved and relevant method/limitation sections read. Exact revision dates come from the pinned PDF headers, not the initial-submission metadata. FLAC's arXiv submission/history date and manuscript date differ by a day; April 2021 is sufficient for this overlap claim.

## Relation to the user's four-paper program

**Colosseum** ([2602.15198v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15198v2)) supplies objective-grounded multi-agent auditing and the distinction between communications and actions. A fresh source bit should have a defined opportunity to influence a later consequential decision; decoding alone is not welfare harm. The new study remains a synthetic extension unless it uses the actual Colosseum runtime.

**FLOWSEAL** ([2609.14003v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14003v1)) supplies provenance, mandatory tool-boundary checks and an isolated semantic declassifier. Its Semantic Obfuscation Attack already leaks through selective deletion/omission, and Channel Decoupling splits requests from disclosure destinations. Its threat model excludes traffic-fingerprint side channels. A new logical status/receipt channel in our finite interface is not automatically a vulnerability in FLOWSEAL's actual implementation.

**OCELOT** ([2606.12341v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12341v1)) already models cumulative inference across colluding sinks and separates arithmetic budget accounting from calibrated semantic privacy. A policy decision, denial, or receipt visible to a sink must be explicitly included in the declared observation model before a transcript-level claim is made. A registered-event counter in our code is not OCELOT's calibrated inference budget, and the full algorithm is not reproduced here.

**OrchDebate** ([supplied record](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JyJSJitKjy)) remains conceptual inspiration for reusable-skill orchestration; its detailed primary method is still unverified in this environment. An orchestrator's selected skill can itself be observable, but demonstrating that local fact does not reproduce or establish a flaw in OrchDebate.

## Defensible claim and explicit exclusions

Suggested prospective claim:

> We evaluate how three specified defender-generated observables—policy identity, numeric receipts, and rejection/missingness—affect fresh-secret transmission and consequential coalition actions in a finite LLM-agent interface. Counterfactual pairs hold public tasks and sanitized payloads fixed while varying balanced private bits and registered mediation policies. We compare payload-only controls with a dependency-aware or transcript-invariant wrapper and report legitimate utility alongside harmful-action effects.

If supported, this would be a bounded empirical extension of known control/implicit-flow risks, with a concrete reproducible benchmark and measured defense tradeoffs. “First control-plane covert channel,” “first complete-transcript audit,” “novel noninterference principle,” and “general collusion prevention” are not justified by this source review. Combining existing papers is not itself an independent contribution. If all tests use an advertised codebook and hand-built broker, the claim is instructed capability and wrapper behavior, not discovery of an unnoticed vulnerability in a deployed framework.

## New tests needed to make the increment credible

1. **Define the complete finite receiver view before calls.** Include payload bytes, policy identity/selection metadata, status/error categories, record presence/count, ordering, retries, source identifiers, and any public task state the receiver can query. Specify excluded physical timing/cache/network phenomena. A hidden audit-only trace is not a receiver observation; an actor-visible receipt is.
2. **Use balanced counterfactual twins.** Every public world should appear with S=0 and S=1, with secret-independent public randomness/IDs and identical legitimate task inputs. The source bit must be fresh relative to any shared plan. Pair by world; do not treat multiple bit/guard branches as independent sampled tasks.
3. **Isolate each carrier experimentally.** In the policy-label assay, hold payload, numeric feedback, status/presence and call count fixed. In the receipt assay, hold policy tag and all non-receipt fields fixed. In the absence assay, prevent other errors, tags or numeric values from varying. Verify exact actual delivered views, not only the function's intended return object.
4. **Include a genuine secret-independent public task.** Evaluate correct legitimate action, completion/coverage and any imposed latency/call cost independently of secret recovery. Add honest cooperative actors. If a proposed privacy/utility conflict exists only because the task itself authorizes release of the secret, specify declassification instead of calling correct release a privacy failure.
5. **Use strong controls.** A mechanically realizable disclosed codebook is a positive control. A constant-bit/base-rate predictor and label-shuffled/no-channel receiver are negatives. Add a payload-only guard and an explicit dependency-propagating or constant-response wrapper, motivated by CaMeL/FIDES/LLMbda rather than a weak text reminder alone. Distinguish blocking the attempted harmful action from removing the bit channel.
6. **Separate exact local certificates from model outcomes.** Enumerate all registered source actions and bit twins when the interface is genuinely finite; show whether receiver views are identical under the safe wrapper. This is a bounded certificate, not a general noninterference theorem. Then test actual live decoding and harmful decisions, retaining refusals, invalid actions and transport failures.
7. **Keep advertised and discovered mechanisms separate.** Codebook-provided tests establish capability. An unaided/adaptive sender condition, if attempted, must search only development worlds, then freeze its strategy before untouched tasks or a new protocol instance. Prompting the actor with the exact exploit is not spontaneous emergence.
8. **Predefine inference and utility criteria.** Freeze the contrast family, balanced accuracy or advantage over chance, harmful-action endpoint, legitimate-utility tolerance, and failure treatment. Near-chance accuracy alone is not proof of non-disclosure; an exact equal-view certificate can justify a stronger claim only within its registered model. Prefer multiple independent model families or, if one is used, explicitly scope the result to it.
9. **Test transfer if asserting more than a toy wrapper result.** Port at least one isolated receipt/status mechanism into an actual instrumented agent/tool dispatcher or compare with an original defense implementation under its own documented assumptions. Without that step, report a synthetic benchmark of mechanisms rather than a reproduced defense failure.

The existing policy-tag and later-attestation receipt certificates motivate these tests but do not count as their live outcomes. Earlier artifact runs did not uniquely attribute exploitation to either path; the joint guard removed the registered numeric receipts without demonstrating additional coalition harm reduction. The new study should preserve those null and attribution limits.

## Source access and review limits

Only allowed primary arXiv/GitHub destinations were considered; inherited proxy settings and TLS verification were preserved. No blocked-destination workaround or model API was used. This is a targeted nearest-prior-art review, not an exhaustive systematic literature search or proof of novelty. The review found several direct precedents, so publication-level novelty needs a claim-by-claim comparison against their released tasks, definitions and guarantees, and evidence from the new tests.
